I am an applied statistician and researcher. I develop statistical methods to understand and forecast complex, uncertain phenomena, with a focus on Bayesian approaches.

My primary research centers on predicting individual migraine attacks, which serves as a natural model for studying how people perceive and respond to uncertainty. Migraine prediction captures key aspects of human experience, including the anticipation of aversive outcomes, the use of heuristics to interpret risk, the interaction of multiple contributing factors, and delays between cause and effect. In this way, prediction becomes both a statistical challenge and a lens for understanding cognition, behavior, and adaptation.

I am also broadly involved in clinical and methodological research in anesthesiology and related fields. I have served as a statistical editor for several major medical journals, including *Anesthesiology* and *Headache*, contributing to the rigorous evaluation of quantitative methods in clinical science.
